# Memo: Where Olist loses customers on delivery, and what to do first

**To:** Marketplace operations and seller-growth leads | **From:** Seller Acquisition & Delivery Diagnostics | **Draft:** 2026-10-15
**Data:** Olist public sample, 96,203 delivered orders (Jan 2017 to Aug 2018) and 8,000 marketing leads. All results are associations; there is no cost data. Details and sources: `docs/findings.md`.

## Bottom line
About 1 order in 15 (6.8%) arrives late, and late orders are far more likely to get a 1-star review. The biggest and most fixable cause is the **carrier leg to a few destinations**, not seller dispatch. Seller dispatch is a smaller second lever. The seller funnel looks healthy and should not be cut back.

## What we found
- **Carrier transit is about 87% of the extra time on late orders;** seller dispatch is about 13%. The idea that most delay starts at the seller is not supported.
- **Delay is concentrated.** Rio de Janeiro and Bahia run about 12% late against 4.5% in Sao Paulo. Sao Paulo to Rio alone is 14.3% late and makes up 17.6% of all late orders.
- **Peak months get worse inside the same states.** In Nov 2017, Feb and Mar 2018 the late rate was 15.2% against 4.5% in other months, and almost none of that came from a change in order mix.
- **Late means unhappy.** 1-star reviews: 6.6% on time, 25% when 1-3 days late, 70% when 8+ days late. Part of the gap is timing (many reviews are written before delivery); counting only reviews written after delivery, the gap is 12 points, not 47.
- **Sellers missing the shipping deadline** are 4.8 times as likely to be late and account for 22% of late orders.
- **Sellers won through the funnel** have better on-time rates, faster dispatch and 17.9 points higher month-2 retention than other sellers (selection effects possible). Paid search converts at least as well as organic (12.3% vs 11.8%); social and email convert at roughly half the rate or less.

## Recommended actions
1. **Prioritise the carrier leg to Rio and Bahia and the peak-season capacity of that leg.** Bringing SP to RJ to the level of SP to MG would avoid roughly 700 late orders (about 11% of all late orders; scenario, not a forecast).
2. **Put the sellers that miss shipping deadlines on a dispatch scorecard.** Moving the worst 10% of eligible sellers (61 sellers) to the median dispatch time avoids about 540 late orders (8%) and 66 to 256 one-star reviews.
3. **Keep funnel investment; do not move effort from paid to organic.** Test whether social and email follow-up is worth the SDR time once lead costs are known.

## What this does not tell you
No costs, margins or return on spend. No proof that fixing delay raises reviews, or that the funnel makes sellers better. The paid-versus-organic first-sale gap (+15.9 points) is exploratory and sits on a p = 0.046 boundary. Only 380 of the 842 deal sellers appear in the order data.
